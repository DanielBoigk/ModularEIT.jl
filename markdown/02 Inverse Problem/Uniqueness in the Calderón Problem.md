**Question.** Does $\Lambda_{\gamma_1} = \Lambda_{\gamma_2}$ imply $\gamma_1 = \gamma_2$ for isotropic conductivities?

Main results, in chronological order:

| Year | Authors | Setting |
|:--|:--|:--|
| 1980 | Calderón | injectivity of the linearisation at constant $\gamma$ |
| 1984–85 | Kohn, Vogelius | [[Boundary Determination|boundary values]] of smooth $\gamma$ and all derivatives; real-analytic and piecewise analytic $\gamma$ |
| 1987 | Sylvester, Uhlmann | $n\ge3$, smooth $\gamma$ ($C^\infty$), via [[Complex Geometrical Optics Solutions]] |
| 1988 | Nachman; Novikov | $n\ge 3$, reconstruction procedures (see [[Reconstruction Algorithms with Guarantees]]) |
| 1996 | Nachman | $n=2$, $\gamma\in W^{2,p}$, $p>1$, constructive ([[D-bar Method]]) |
| 1997 | Brown, Uhlmann | $n=2$, $\gamma\in W^{1,p}$, $p > 2$ |
| 2006 | Astala, Päivärinta | $n=2$, $\gamma\in L^\infty$ (only bounded above and below) |
| 2013 | Haberman, Tataru | $n\ge 3$, $\gamma\in C^1$, or Lipschitz and close to constant |
| 2015 | Haberman | $n=3,4$, $\gamma\in W^{1,n}$ |
| 2016 | Caro, Rogers | $n\ge 3$, $\gamma$ Lipschitz |

The two-dimensional problem is special. There the conductivity equation can be rewritten as a Beltrami equation, and quasiconformal mapping techniques give uniqueness in $L^\infty$. In dimension $n\ge 3$, whether $L^\infty$ conductivities are uniquely determined is still open.

Uniqueness fails for [[Anisotropic Conductivities]]: they are determined at most up to a boundary-fixing diffeomorphism.
