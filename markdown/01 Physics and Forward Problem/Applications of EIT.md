[[Electrical Impedance Tomography]] is used wherever conductivity contrasts show up and a cheap, fast, non-invasive measurement matters more than fine spatial resolution.

**Medical**
- Bedside monitoring of lung ventilation and perfusion. This is the most established clinical use: air-filled lung tissue conducts much worse than blood and other tissue.
- Detecting and classifying stroke, monitoring brain activity.
- Breast cancer screening, since tumours often differ in conductivity from healthy tissue.
- Gastric emptying and monitoring of bladder volume.

**Industrial and geophysical**
- Monitoring multiphase flow in pipes and process tomography of mixing vessels.
- Detecting leaks and corrosion.
- Non-destructive testing of concrete and other structures (damage and crack detection).
- Electrical resistivity tomography (ERT) of the subsurface. It is the same inverse problem with electrodes on the ground surface or in boreholes.
