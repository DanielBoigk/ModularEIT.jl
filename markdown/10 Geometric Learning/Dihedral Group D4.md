The **dihedral group $D_4$** is the symmetry group of the square. It is the subgroup of $O(2)$ that maps a square pixel grid onto itself. It has 8 elements, generated by a rotation $r$ by $90°$ and a reflection $f$:

$$
D_4 = \{e,\ r,\ r^2,\ r^3,\ f,\ rf,\ r^2f,\ r^3f\},\qquad r^4 = f^2 = e,\qquad fr = r^{-1}f = r^3f .
$$

**Cayley table** (row $\cdot$ column):

| $\cdot$ | $e$ | $r$ | $r^2$ | $r^3$ | $f$ | $rf$ | $r^2f$ | $r^3f$ |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| **$e$** | $e$ | $r$ | $r^2$ | $r^3$ | $f$ | $rf$ | $r^2f$ | $r^3f$ |
| **$r$** | $r$ | $r^2$ | $r^3$ | $e$ | $rf$ | $r^2f$ | $r^3f$ | $f$ |
| **$r^2$** | $r^2$ | $r^3$ | $e$ | $r$ | $r^2f$ | $r^3f$ | $f$ | $rf$ |
| **$r^3$** | $r^3$ | $e$ | $r$ | $r^2$ | $r^3f$ | $f$ | $rf$ | $r^2f$ |
| **$f$** | $f$ | $r^3f$ | $r^2f$ | $rf$ | $e$ | $r^3$ | $r^2$ | $r$ |
| **$rf$** | $rf$ | $f$ | $r^3f$ | $r^2f$ | $r$ | $e$ | $r^3$ | $r^2$ |
| **$r^2f$** | $r^2f$ | $rf$ | $f$ | $r^3f$ | $r^2$ | $r$ | $e$ | $r^3$ |
| **$r^3f$** | $r^3f$ | $r^2f$ | $rf$ | $f$ | $r^3$ | $r^2$ | $r$ | $e$ |

**Structure.** $D_4$ is non-abelian. It contains the cyclic rotation subgroup $C_4 = \{e,r,r^2,r^3\}$ (index 2, normal) and four reflections: two across the axes and two across the diagonals. It can also be generated by two reflections, for example $f$ and the transpose (reflection across a diagonal). Their product is a rotation.

**Action on filters.** On a $(2k+1)\times(2k+1)$ filter centred at the origin, $D_4$ acts by rotating and flipping the array (`rot90`, `reverse`, `transpose`). For a point at offset $(i,j)$, the orbit has

- size 1 for the centre,
- size 4 for points on an axis ($j = 0$) or a diagonal ($|i| = |j|$),
- size 8 otherwise.

This orbit structure determines the dimension of invariant filter spaces (see [[Invariant Filter Banks]]).
